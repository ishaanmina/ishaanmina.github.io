The sun's corona is only observable from the ground during a total solar eclipse, and the totality window
at Exmouth on 20 April 2023 was about two minutes long. Everything — mounts, tracking, filters, power,
capture routines — had to work on the first attempt, in the field, with no second run.

### Hardware

- Designed and manufactured custom telescope mounts, dew-heater power banks, camera filters and supporting
  hardware using 3D printing and laser cutting.
- Travelled to Exmouth as **equipment lead** for the expedition.

### Capture

- Captured imagery across **4 telescopes**, each running a different capture algorithm, at **30 fps** per
  camera through the full ~2-minute totality.
- The mounts and tracking performed as intended, holding the sun stationary in frame for the entire window,
  with a different filter per scope capturing a different image type.
- Applied photometry techniques on site; the custom 3D-printed filters enabled capture of low-intensity
  solar spot changes.

### Why it matters

Coronal imagery is used to map the sun's atmosphere and to anticipate coronal mass ejections and solar wind
events — the same space-weather events that disrupt satellites, power grids and Earth-based electronics.

### Skills

Mechanical design for field conditions · 3D printing · laser cutting · optical filter design · telescope mount and tracking design · astrophotography capture pipelines · photometry
