**50+ bachelor students** · Formula Student junior engineer training

A Formula Student team rebuilds itself every year. Seniors graduate, and the knowledge required to build a
400 V electric racecar has to be transferred to a new intake in time for them to be useful before the next
competition. I ran that training for 50+ juniors as part of the Accumulator Department.

The format was classroom lectures paired with hands-on work, because neither half works alone — a lecture
on solder joints produces students who cannot solder, and unguided hands-on time produces confident
students who are wrong.

### Electrical

- **PCB design** — schematic capture through to layout, and the design decisions that make a board
  debuggable rather than merely correct.
- **Soldering** — through-hole and surface mount, including the failure modes that pass visual inspection.
- **Measurement** — using instruments properly, and knowing what a reading is actually telling you.
- **Battery modelling methods** — how cells are characterised and represented, and why the model you choose
  determines what your pack-level predictions are worth.
- **Charging methods** — charging profiles for lithium-ion chemistry and the constraints behind them.
- **Battery pack design** — pack topologies and the trade-offs between them.
- **Balancing algorithms** — passive and active cell balancing, and why a pack without balancing degrades to
  the capacity of its weakest cell.

### Mechanical

- **CAD** — modelling parts that can actually be manufactured, not just rendered.
- **Fasteners** — bolt selection, grades, preload, and why the fastener is so often the part that fails.
- **Mechanical joints** — joint types, load paths, and choosing between them.
- **Heat dispersion** — thermal management, which on an electric car is a design constraint on the
  accumulator rather than an afterthought.

### Why this content

Nearly all of it maps directly onto failure modes the team had actually hit. Cell balancing, bolt preload
and thermal management are not abstract syllabus topics in a Formula Student context — they are the
specific things that end a season at technical inspection.
