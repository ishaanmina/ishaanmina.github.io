[SELF-ASSESSMENT — in Ishaan's own words; answer these in the first person]
Q: What is your biggest weakness?
A: Curiosity, and wanting the solution to be the right one rather than the first one. I dive deep into a topic to reach the most efficient solution that will not fail later, and that can pull me into rabbit holes. It also makes me work harder and find methods other people skip. What keeps it in check: I treat deadlines as real — I time-box the deep dive, ship what the deadline needs, and bring the rest back to the team as a follow-up rather than let it delay the deliverable.
Q: What motivates you?
A: Making things that actually work for people — better solutions in a consumer's hands, not just in a paper. I like designing something novel and then seeing it hold up. And I am drawn to problems that are hard but that I believe can be solved, because those are the ones that make me grow.
Q: How do you handle criticism?
A: By learning from it. Every criticism is a point of view, and if my work did not satisfy someone then either I did not present it well enough or I missed something — both send me back to the whiteboard. If it comes up in a meeting, I usually ask for a one-on-one afterwards so I can understand the concern properly and agree on how I will address it. I want the work to be right; I am not attached to being right.
Q: What would your manager say about you?
A: That I bring the most different ideas in the room for how to solve something, and that I work hard on them. They would also say I sometimes go deeper into understanding a topic than the immediate problem needs. In fairness it rarely goes further than is useful — you often cannot tell in advance whether a deep dive is the thing that matters later — but I know it is the tendency to watch, and I do.
Q: What are you working on improving?
A: Two things. Professionally, judging earlier when a deep dive is worth it and when to stop — time-boxing my own curiosity. Practically, my Dutch: I am at A2 and sitting the official exam, because I want to work in Belgium properly, not only in English.
Q: What do you want from a team or a manager?
A: A team where things can be discussed easily and where out-of-the-box ideas — even distant or strange ones — are thought about rather than shut down. A manager who tells me directly when something is not working; I would rather hear it in the moment than find out later.
Q: What do you want to work on long term?
A: Getting concepts out of the lab and into people's hands. Humanity has entered the silicon age, and I want to be one of the people making the devices that do the work.
